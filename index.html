(09) Why reward points?

Closing thetiming gap

Wellness has a timing problem: health benefits arrive weeks later, but the effort is needed today. Hadipoints close that gap with an instant reward for each healthy action.

The key decision was what points buy. Instead of generic discounts, they're redeemed in the Hadi market for more wellness, such as guided sessions and company events. Every reward leads to another healthy action.

Habit and rewards loop
(10) Key decisions

Three choices shaped the product more than any others.

Small, frequentwins

Trade-off

Why 5-minute sessions instead of full workouts?

Longer programs deliver bigger gains per session, but research showed people wouldn't start them during the workday. We chose small, frequent wins over big, rare ones, because consistency creates lasting habits.

Pilot: 4 sessions a week
Motivation

Why team challenges?

Motivation fades when you're alone. Turning steps into a shared journey to Dubai landmarks gave colleagues a reason to check in on each other.

Pilot: 3 of 6 joined
Trust

Why smartphone detox?

Workplace stress often comes from constant notifications, not just workload. A detox feature inside a phone app tells users Hadify cares about their wellbeing more than their screen time, and that trust brings them back.

Designing for real life
  • A company code links each employee to their workplace at sign-up; 5 of 6 testers finished onboarding this way.
  • Clear password guidance prevents early frustration.
  • Empty states encourage action when there are no events yet.
  • Notifications let users decide when to act.

(09) Why a closed loop?

Closing theaction gap

HR's real problem wasn't missing data. It was the gap between seeing a problem and doing something about it. Each step lived in a different place: scores in one report, emails in another, events in a spreadsheet.

I designed the dashboard as one loop. It flags the team at risk, the AI assistant explains why, HR sends that team a challenge with reward points, and reports show what changed. Every insight leads to an action, and every action leads back to evidence.

Spot · Ask · Act · Measure
(10) Key decisions

Three choices shaped the dashboard more than any others.

Insight thatleads to action

Trade-off

Why burnout risk by department, not one company score?

A single score is simpler to read, but research showed it hid IT's burnout inside the average. I chose depth by team over a one-number view and accepted a denser screen, then used clear hierarchy and an Average mode tab to keep it readable.

Research: IT flagged
AI

Why an AI assistant inside the dashboard?

HR and managers aren't data analysts. Charts show what changed, rarely why. Emi sits right where the question comes up, so HR can ask which team performed low, why, and how to improve that team's wellbeing, without leaving the page.

Plain-language answers
Business

Why put challenge creation next to the data?

An insight without an action is just a worry. Department targeting and HP rewards on every challenge turn a red number into a response in minutes, and every response creates new data that proves the program's value to leadership.

Designing for real life
  • Each company signs in to its own branded space, so the tool feels like theirs from the first screen.
  • Inline errors in forms show exactly which field needs fixing before HR hits Create.
  • Paused and “Not planned yet” states in the schedule tell HR what to do next.
  • Single-day and date-range pickers, plus registration windows, match how real events run.
  • Department restrictions and participant limits let HR target events precisely.
  • Export on every table, because leadership reports still live outside the tool.
